    Method

    How this is calculated

    • Assessed value = market value × 11.5% (Kansas residential assessment rate).
    • Tax = assessed value × total mills ÷ 1,000. A mill is $1 per $1,000 of assessed value.
    • School exemption. Every school district rate on the levy sheet includes the 20.000-mill statewide school levy. Kansas exempts part of a home's appraised value from that 20-mill levy only (K.S.A. 79-201x; $75,000 since tax year 2024 under 2024 Special Session SB 1, previously $40,000). The tool subtracts value × 11.5% × 20 ÷ 1,000 for the exempt amount from the school district line. You can change or turn off the exemption under Options on the first page.
    • Tax units. A tax unit is an area where the same set of taxing districts overlaps, so every home in it has the same rate. ZIP codes do not follow these lines; most ZIPs contain several tax units. The “share of ZIP area” is a share of land area, not of homes, so a large rural unit can lead the list even where most homes are in a city.
    • Rates. Each unit's rate adds the 2025 levies (official County Clerk sheet, levied in November 2025 for 2026 budgets) of the State (1.500), Sedgwick County (27.567), County Fire District 1 (16.754, where it applies), the city, the school district (including any recreation commission), the township, and any library, cemetery, drainage, improvement, watershed, or hospital district. Computed totals match the sheet's published total for each city's main tax unit.
    • County functions. The County dollars use the County rate in your tax unit. They are split across the five County levy funds and then by function. The default uses the 2027 Recommended Budget's fund mix (General Fund 23.156, WSU 1.500, Aging 0.330, Highways 0.855, Bond & Interest 1.001; total 26.842 mills), scaled to the 2025 County rate. The General Fund share is split by the General Fund's 2027 “Expenditures by Functional Area” (2027 Budget Summary by Function, $363.9 million). The alternative uses the 2025 levy sheet's actual fund rates. County Fire District 1 is a separate taxing district and is shown under Fire, not here.

    Caveats

    • This is an estimate, not a tax bill or an official County calculation. It is not affiliated with Sedgwick County.
    • It excludes special assessments, other exemptions and credits (for example, homestead refunds), appraisal differences, and changes in rates after 2025.
    • Districts with no 2025 levy line on the sheet (some drainage, improvement, and watershed districts and Jamesburg Park Cemetery) are counted as zero. TIF and transportation district areas use the regular rates.
    • General Fund spending by function includes interfund transfers, and spending is funded by sales tax, fees, and other revenue as well as property tax. The function split is a proportional allocation, not a tracing of your dollars.
