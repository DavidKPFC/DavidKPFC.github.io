Research

The research follows three tracks. Each starts from a problem that budget and finance officers face, and each returns to what they can do about it.

Property tax and tax limitations

First, the property tax remains the main own-source revenue of local governments, and states continue to limit it. This track studies assessment caps, portability, homestead exemptions, and tax and expenditure limits, and what they do to local revenue and to the distribution of the tax burden.

  • Guo and Frank (2015), “Portability, an Innovative Property Tax Relief Whose Time Hasn’t Come,” Journal of Public Budgeting, Accounting & Financial Management.
  • Guo (2011), “The Effects of Tax and Expenditure Limits on State Discretionary Tax Adjustment,” Journal of Public Budgeting, Accounting & Financial Management.
  • Guo and Cheng (2025), Fiscal Structure and Revenue Resilience of Florida Municipalities, a report for the Florida League of Cities. See the Florida Property Tax Study.

Fiscal stress, reserves, and forecasting

Second, revenue shocks force governments to cut spending, raise revenue, or draw on reserves. This track studies fund balances and rainy day funds, revenue forecasting, and the choice of budget-gap closing strategies.

  • Guo and Wang (2017), “A Spatial Analysis of Florida County Governments’ Unreserved General Fund Balances,” Public Budgeting & Finance.
  • Guo and Neshkova (2018), “Fiscal Severity and the Choice of Budget-Gap Closing Strategies,” Public Money & Management.
  • Guo and Chen (2021), “Forecasting Revenue Impacts from COVID-19: The Case of Florida Municipalities,” State and Local Government Review.

Transparency and citizen participation

Third, residents can hold governments accountable only when fiscal information is disclosed and understandable. This track studies financial disclosure, such as tax abatement reporting under GASB Statement 77, and the ways residents take part in budgeting, including budget simulations.

  • Frank, Guo, and Cong (2024), “Disclosure of Tax Abatement: State Governments’ Compliance with GASB Statement 77,” Municipal Finance Journal.
  • Guo and Neshkova (2013), “Citizen Input in the Budget Process: When Does It Matter Most?” American Review of Public Administration.
  • Mohr, Guo, Porter, Owens, Poe Rosseau, and Afonso (2026), “Structuring Simulations for Local Government Resident Engagement,” Public Finance Journal.
